One decode step is 29.7 ms — about 29.7M L1 cache references, or 59 round trips across a datacenter, spent almost entirely waiting for 70.6 GB of weights to come back from HBM. Five hundred more of them — about 14.8 s — and you have an answer.

Order-of-magnitude only — dense tensor-core rates and published bandwidths, with the derates listed under Method below. Treat mixture-of-experts at batch 1 as optimistic and everything else as ±2×.

Method

How the numbers are made

A decode step costs whichever is slower, reading the weights or doing the arithmetic, plus the collectives, which do not overlap with either. KV cache shards across ranks until every rank owns one key-value head and then stops. A mixture-of-experts layer reads only the experts its batch happens to touch, but all of them still have to be resident. A diffusion transformer attends in both directions, so its attention term is quadratic and not halved. Everything else is a spec sheet and a derate.

Derates

  • HBM read efficiency80% of the spec sheet
  • Usable HBM90% of capacity
  • MFU55% prefill, 75% decode
  • Kernel launch120 µs per step, CUDA-graphed
  • NVLink hop3 µs, half the bidirectional rate
  • InfiniBand hop9 µs, 50 GB/s per GPU
  • NVMe offload14 GB/s per GPU
  • Chunked prefill512-token chunks

What it does not model

Paged-attention fragmentation, prefix-cache hits, speculative decoding, and pipeline or expert parallelism as separate axes — every GPU count here is tensor-parallel. Mixture of experts at batch 1 is the weakest case and reads optimistic; batched serving, the regime anyone actually deploys, lands within about a factor of two of published measurements.
