Toy simulator

A roofline you can drag

Pick a model, a number format, and an accelerator. This works out how many GPUs are needed to hold the thing, how long one token takes, and which piece of hardware you are actually waiting on — weights crossing HBM, tensor cores, the fabric, or a disk. It is a napkin, not a benchmark. But the shape of the answer is right, and the shape is the part worth arguing about.

Where this configuration sits

The roof is H200 at FP8; the two faint lines are the other accelerators for scale. Anything left of the ridge is waiting on memory no matter how good the kernel is.

One decode step is 29.7 ms — about 29.7M L1 cache references, or 59 round trips across a datacenter, spent almost entirely waiting for 70.6 GB of weights to come back from HBM. Five hundred more of them — about 14.8 s — and you have an answer.

Order-of-magnitude only. Dense (non-sparse) tensor-core rates, HBM read at 80% of spec, MFU 55% for prefill, one CUDA-graph replay per step, ring all-reduce, NDR InfiniBand at 50 GB/s per GPU, one PCIe Gen5 NVMe drive per GPU at 14 GB/s. It does not model paged-attention fragmentation, prefix-cache hits, speculative decoding, or rack-scale expert parallelism — so treat MoE at batch 1 as optimistic and everything else as ±2×.

Method

How the numbers are made

Nothing here is measured. Every figure below comes from a published spec sheet and a few lines of arithmetic, written out so you can disagree with a specific step rather than with the result.

Footprint

Weights
P × bytes_per_weight, for the whole model — MoE experts included, since capacity is dense even when compute is sparse
SSD offload
weights are evicted first, so the evicted share is a fraction of the weight tensor; a shortfall larger than the weights is KV cache that has to be paged in too. One PCIe Gen5 drive per GPU at 14 GB/s
KV cache, MHA and GQA
2 × kv_heads × head_dim × layers × bytes, per token
KV cache, MLA
(kv_lora_rank + qk_rope_head_dim) × layers × bytes = 576 × layers, per token
KV cache, DiT
none — a diffusion transformer attends over the whole clip in both directions and re-derives K and V at every step
Activations
6% of weights + 1.5 GB for a language model; 8 × batch × tokens × d_model × 2 B for a video model, where the attention working set dominates
GPU count
the smallest power of two whose combined HBM, derated to 90% for allocator overhead and fragmentation, holds the total

One output token

Step time
max(memory, compute) + collectives + launch + storage — memory and math overlap, the fabric and the drive do not
Memory
(weight bytes read ÷ N + KV bytes read) ÷ (HBM bandwidth × 0.8), the derate being what a good decode kernel actually sustains
Memory, MoE
the weight term is divided again by an expert-GEMM efficiency that climbs from 0.3 to 1 as tokens-per-expert (batch × topk ÷ experts) fills a 96-row tile — scattered per-expert GEMMs do not stream at full rate
Weight bytes, MoE
dense + routed × (1 − (1 − topk/experts)^batch) — an expert is read if any token in the decode batch routes to it
KV bytes under tensor parallelism
divided by min(N, kv_heads) for MHA and GQA — head-parallel sharding stops once every rank owns one kv head. MLA has a single latent that TP would have to replicate, which is why DeepSeek runs attention data-parallel instead; sequences split, so it shards across all N
Compute
(2 × active_params × batch + 4 × layers × batch × context × d_attn) ÷ (N × peak × 0.75)
Collectives
two ring all-reduces per layer, each moving 2(N−1)/N × batch × d_model × 2 B, plus a fixed hop latency — 3 µs inside an NVLink domain, 9 µs across InfiniBand
Expert routing
a mixture-of-experts layer adds a dispatch and a combine all-to-all, each moving (batch ÷ N) × (1 − 1/N) × topk × d_model × 2 B per rank
Launch
120 µs for one CUDA-graph replay and the scheduler around it

Time to first token

Prefill FLOPs
2 × active_params × T + 2 × layers × T² × d_attn — one arriving request, not the whole batch, and halved because attention is causal
Prefill weight traffic, MoE
a T-token prompt routes to almost every expert, so the touched fraction uses the prompt length as its exponent, not the decode batch size — prefill reads nearly the whole model where decode reads a slice of it
Prefill time
max(compute at 55% MFU, weight and KV traffic) + all-reduce over T tokens
Chunked prefill
the same work cut into 512-token pieces, so the worst decode stall is one chunk rather than one whole prompt. Chunks are equal in tokens but not in work — the last one attends over the whole prompt, so the worst stall carries the full attention term
Disaggregated prefill
adds one KV cache transfer of the finished sequence — one shard per rank, over NVLink when both pools fit in a node and over InfiniBand when they do not
Static batching
an arriving request also waits out the batch already running, charged as 64 decode steps — a stand-in for a mean output length the simulator does not ask you for
Chunked prefill surcharge
time to first token is 15% above the raw prefill, the cost of interleaving chunks with a live decode batch

Video diffusion

Sequence length
Wan's VAE compresses 4× in time and 8× in space, then the DiT patchifies (1,2,2) — a 5-second 480p clip is 32,760 tokens, a 5-second 1080p clip is 171,360
One denoise step
2 × P × N + 4 × layers × N² × d_attn — the full attention matrix, not half, because a DiT is bidirectional
One clip
one step × denoise steps × 2, the doubling being classifier-free guidance
Denoise-step collectives
the same two all-reduces per layer, but the payload carries the whole clip — 2(N−1)/N × batch × tokens × d_model × 2 B — which is why splitting a video model across nodes stops paying off almost immediately

Roofline

Arithmetic intensity
FLOPs issued ÷ bytes moved, per GPU
Ridge point
peak FLOP/s ÷ HBM bandwidth. Lower precision raises the peak but not the bandwidth, so cheaper math pushes the ridge further right
Emulated formats
a format with no native tensor-core path keeps the memory saving and runs the math at the BF16 rate, minus 15% for in-kernel dequantization

Serving discipline

Each discipline sets a duty cycle — the fraction of the decode loop doing useful work — and a definition of the worst gap between two tokens. Those are the two numbers these papers are actually about.

DisciplineDutyWhat it changes
Static batching45%A batch runs to completion, so finished sequences idle until the longest one ends.
Continuous batching (Orca)85%Iteration-level scheduling. An arriving prefill still runs alone, so the worst inter-token gap is a whole prompt.
Chunked prefill (Sarathi-Serve)90%Prefill rides along with decode in 512-token pieces. The tail stops spiking.
Prefill / decode split92%Separate pools, no interference, one KV transfer per request.

What it does not model

Paged-attention fragmentation, prefix-cache hits, speculative decoding, pipeline and expert parallelism as separate axes — every GPU count here is tensor-parallel — rack-scale routing topology, quantization error, and every scheduler heuristic worth having. Mixture-of-experts at batch 1 is the weakest case and reads optimistic; batched serving, the regime anyone actually deploys, lands within about a factor of two of published measurements.
